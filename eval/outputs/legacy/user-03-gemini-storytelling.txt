Storytelling didn't start with books. It started with people sitting around fires, using chants and cave paintings to pass down what mattered — hunts, myths, survival. Writing systems came later, then the printing press turned books from rare monastic treasures into something ordinary people could actually own. Okay. That shift sparked the Enlightenment. Not a small thing. Then photography, cinema, radio, television — each one added a new layer of how a story could land in your body, not just your head.

And now it's something else entirely. Interactive digital platforms, virtual reality, and algorithmic content generation have changed what "audience" even means — people don't just watch anymore, they co-create narratives and move through worlds shaped around them. That's a real rupture from passive formats. But here's the part that doesn't change, the part that's almost annoying in how consistent it is: the core essence of storytelling remains, well, actually, not entirely unchanged — but close enough that it holds. Whether it's a folk tale someone's grandmother told, or a VR experience built by, well, mostly, an algorithm, stories are still our primary vessel for empathy. Here's what actually happens: we're still just trying to make sense of things — Anyway, that's just one way to think about it.
